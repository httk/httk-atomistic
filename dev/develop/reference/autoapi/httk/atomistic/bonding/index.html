httk.atomistic.bonding¶

Default bond networks from interatomic distances.

distance_bonds() gives a structure that lacks a bond network a simple default one. It uses the radius-sum form of OpenBabel and Jmol (their form, not their exact radii or floor): two atoms are bonded when their distance lies between 0.5 Å and the sum of their covalent radii plus a tolerance (0.45 Å by default). The radii are the single-bond covalent radii of Cordero et al. [Cordero2008] (the low-spin values for Mn, Fe and Co, sp3 for C). On top of it, two metals are not bonded in a structure that contains a non-metal (H, He, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar, Ge, As, Se, Br, Kr, Sb, Te, I, Xe, At, Rn: the metalloids count as non-metals), so the generous radius sums of cations do not bond the cations of ionic solids to each other.

Finally, a metal does not bond to a non-metal across a non-metal ligand they share: such a bond longer than the sum of the two radii is dropped when a non-metal bonded to both is more electronegative than either (Pauling electronegativities as compiled by WebElements [WebElementsEN], mostly Allred’s revised values [Allred1961]), so the large cations of oxo-salts do not bond to the oxo-anion centres.

The network it returns names its rule as its source (software httk_basic, the installed httk-atomistic version), and by default it also assigns the baseline shapes; assign_baseline_shapes() adds those to a network that has none.

distance_bonds() never recognises symmetry itself (an ASUStructureView input resolves its own recognition): a unit cell gets a unit-cell network and an asymmetric unit a network of bond orbits. On a measured cell whose metric keeps the group only approximately the two routes can label one crystal differently: the unit-cell route assigns each atom its own shape and keeps contacts at their measured, float-noisy distances, which recognition may then find not invariant and drop, while the asymmetric-unit route (distance_bonds(ASUStructureView(cell, tolerance=...))) idealises first.

This is a heuristic, not a chemical model: it ignores metal-metal bonds in compounds (Re-Re in Re2Cl8(2-) salts, Hg2(2+)), judges anion-anion contacts by distance alone, and the radii fit covalent bonds better than ionic ones. It takes no account of occupancy, so on a disordered structure the partially occupied atoms that overlap or sit a few tenths of an angstrom apart are all bonded: per-site bond counts above about 60 come from disorder, not coordination (the largest measured were 1522 on ICSD, a mixed O/Ti site of ICSD 162568, and 203 on carbon-free COD).

[Cordero2008]

[Allred1961]

A. L. Allred, “Electronegativity values from thermochemical data”, J. Inorg. Nucl. Chem. 17 (1961) 215-221, doi:10.1016/0022-1902(61)80142-5.

Module Contents¶

httk.atomistic.bonding.distance_bonds(structure, *, tolerance=0.45, shapes=True)[source]¶

Return a structure with the bond network found from interatomic distances, of the kind it was given.

The result keeps the input’s sites and replaces its bonds. It never recognises symmetry itself; an ASUStructureView input resolves its own recognition. A FundamentalDomainStructure or ASUStructure (an ASUStructureView resolved) gives the same class with a network of bond orbits. A unit cell or unit-cell view gives UnitcellStructureView(structure).unview() with explicit pairs between its sites. Raw inputs (a path, a text stream, a storage record) are first adopted as structures; lazy sources (a CIF block or file, a datastream) are resolved and a fundamental-domain record gives its domain, and then these two rules apply. Any other structure gives UnitcellStructureView(structure).unview(), so a NumericUnitcellStructure gives a UnitcellStructure. For the network of the recognised asymmetric unit, recognise first: distance_bonds(ASUStructureView(structure, tolerance=...)). On a measured cell whose metric keeps the group only approximately the two routes can label one crystal differently: the unit-cell route assigns each atom its own shape and keeps contacts at their measured distances, the asymmetric-unit route idealises first.

Two atoms a and b are bonded iff 0.5 <= d(a, b) <= r(a) + r(b) + tolerance (Å), with r the covalent radii of Cordero et al. (2008). A mixed-occupancy site takes the largest radius of its constituents (positive concentration, vacancies and attached atoms ignored); a site with no constituent (only vacancies) gets no bonds. Pairs closer than 0.5 Å (split or disordered positions) are never bonded, so coincident sites are never bonded to each other. In a structure with at least one non-metal (H, He, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar, Ge, As, Se, Br, Kr, Sb, Te, I, Xe, At, Rn), two metal sites are never bonded; a mixed site is a metal when all its constituents are, so even a dilute non-metal on a mixed site switches this rule on for the whole structure. Without a non-metal (intermetallics, elements) the distance rule alone applies. Along a non-periodic direction of a unit cell, atoms are not repeated.

The bonds so found form the tentative network. A metal does not bond to a non-metal across a non-metal ligand they share: a tentative bond between a metal a and a non-metal b is dropped iff d(a, b) > r(a) + r(b) (it exists only through the tolerance) and some non-metal c tentatively bonded to both is more electronegative than both. All drops are decided on the tentative network in one pass. Metal-metal and non-metal-non-metal bonds are never dropped. Electronegativities are Pauling’s as compiled by WebElements (Allred 1961, else Pauling 1960 or Huheey et al. 1993). A mixed site is a metal end when all its constituents are metals and a non-metal ligand when all are non-metals; it counts with its constituents’ largest value as a or b and their smallest as c, so a bond is dropped only when the condition holds for every constituent with a value. A metal end without a value (Pm, Eu, Tb, Yb) counts as less electronegative than any non-metal with one: a mixed metal end ignores its constituents without a value when taking the largest, and counts so when none has one. Any other site with a constituent without a value (He, Ne, Ar, Rn, or one of those metals beside a non-metal on a mixed site) never acts as b or c. No values are invented. This parts the large cations of oxo-salts from the anion centres (in diopside CaMgSi2O6, Ca from Si: Ca 8 and Si 4 bonds, not 12 and 6).

A heuristic: it misses metal-metal bonds in compounds (Re-Re in Re2Cl8(2-) salts, Hg2(2+)) and judges anion-anion contacts by distance only. The shared-ligand rule cannot part a cation from an anion centre closer than their radius sum (Cs, Rb and Ba radii are large) or sharing no more electronegative non-metal; and it drops real metal-ligand bonds longer than the radius sum that share a cage or ring atom (C, N or S) more electronegative than both, as in metallacarboranes (Fe-B 2.23-2.38 Å across cage C), thiaboranes (Ir-B across S) and eta-bonded heteroarenes (Fe-C across N).

Distances are floats computed from the exact structure: the network is the same for every exact re-description of one crystal, except that a pair within float noise of its cutoff or of its radius sum may fall on either side.

The network’s source names the rule: software httk_basic, the installed httk-atomistic version and a one-sentence description with tolerance. With shapes (the default) every site of two or more bonds also gets its baseline shape (see assign_baseline_shapes()), with shapes_source None (the same source).

Example:

>>> from httk.atomistic import ASUStructure, Cell, Species, WyckoffSite, distance_bonds
>>> nacl = ASUStructure(
...     Cell([[5.64, 0, 0], [0, 5.64, 0], [0, 0, 5.64]]), 225,
...     [WyckoffSite("a", (), "Na"), WyckoffSite("b", (), "Cl")],
...     [Species("Na", ("Na",), (1,)), Species("Cl", ("Cl",), (1,))],
... )
>>> bonded = distance_bonds(nacl)  # one bond orbit: Na-Cl at 2.82 Å
>>> len(bonded.bonds.pairs), bonded.bonds.shapes
(1, ('OC6', 'OC6'))
Parameters:

  • structure (httk.atomistic.models.structure.like.StructureLike) – Any structure.

  • tolerance (float) – Å added to the sum of the radii.

  • shapes (bool) – Whether to assign the baseline shapes as well.

Returns:

The structure, of the kind described above, with its distance-based network.

Raises:

ValueError – If a site holds an element without a radius (named in the message), tolerance is not a finite number, or (with shapes) the baseline cannot assign a shape, as for a fundamental-domain site at a special point of its own Wyckoff letter; that message says to pass shapes=False.

Return type:

httk.atomistic.models.structure.asu.FundamentalDomainStructure | httk.atomistic.models.structure.unitcell.UnitcellStructure

httk.atomistic.bonding.assign_baseline_shapes(structure)[source]¶

Return a structure whose bond network has no shapes with its baseline shapes assigned.

The structure is taken as distance_bonds() takes it (a fundamental domain stays one, anything else is presented as a unit cell, and symmetry is never recognised by the function itself). Every site of two or more bonds gets the shape whose ideal sorted bond angles fit its own best (IUPAC and SHAPE symbols, I (irregular) above 15 degrees RMS misfit, L2 from 165 degrees; see httk.atomistic.symmetry.shapes), and shapes_source names the baseline (software httk_basic, the installed httk-atomistic version); the pairs and source are kept. When no site gets a shape (no site has two bonds) the structure is returned with its network unchanged.

Parameters:

structure (httk.atomistic.models.structure.like.StructureLike) – A structure with a bond network and no shapes.

Returns:

The structure, of the kind distance_bonds() returns, with the shapes assigned.

Raises:

ValueError – If the structure has no network, its network already has shapes, or the baseline cannot assign a shape (a fundamental-domain site at a special point of its own Wyckoff letter).

Return type:

httk.atomistic.models.structure.asu.FundamentalDomainStructure | httk.atomistic.models.structure.unitcell.UnitcellStructure
