httk.atomistic.symmetry.shapes¶

Baseline shape detector for the bonds around one atom (private; protonet follow-up 5, part B).

A site’s shape is the arrangement of the bonds of one of its atoms: the directions from the atom to every atom it is bonded to. The baseline detector looks at directions only, not bond lengths:

  1. take the angle between every pair of bond directions and sort the angles;

  2. for every ideal shape with the same number of bonds, the misfit is the root mean square difference between the sorted angles and the ideal shape’s sorted angles (degrees), rounded to 1e-6;

  3. the shape with the smallest misfit wins (ties: table order); a misfit above _IRREGULAR_ABOVE gives I;

  4. two bonds: L2 if the angle is at least _LINEAR_FROM, else A2;

  5. a bond count without ideal shapes gives I; fewer than two bonds give no shape.

This is a port of the workspace reference docs/protonet_followup/shape_reference.py and must reproduce it exactly (floats: geometry presentation only). Some ideal shapes lie closer to each other than the irregular limit in this measure (COC12/ACOC12 9.38, COC12/IC12 11.70, TP3/TPY3 13.00, CU8/HBPY8 14.49 degrees RMS); the closest shape still wins deterministically.

Symbols are IUPAC polyhedral symbols without the hyphen where IUPAC defines one [IUPAC], the SHAPE program’s labels otherwise [SHAPE], and I (irregular) of our own:

The shapes that predate follow-up 7 keep the reference’s own geometries (for example the trigonal pyramid at 107 degrees between bonds). Three of them deliberately differ from SHAPE’s reference coordinates [cosymlib]: SPY5 (square pyramid at 100 degrees between apex and base) is 5.04 degrees from SHAPE SPY-5; TPRS9 (prism at 45 degrees from the axis) is 3.28 degrees from TCTPR-9; and PPY6 (an icosahedron vertex with its five neighbours and one outward bond) is not SHAPE’s PPY-6, whose coordinates are detected as TPR6 (14.56). TPRS7 is SHAPE’s reference geometry CTPR-7, taken about the central atom from the program’s coordinates [cosymlib] (see also [Casanova2003]); TPRT8 and HBPY9 are the undistorted constructions, which equal SHAPE’s ETBPY-8 and HBPY-9.

The table is what httk recommends and what the detector assigns. Some IUPAC shapes are left out of it: this measure barely tells each from a shape of the table, which its ideal geometry is detected as and which is its class. SPY4 (four-bond square pyramid, 11.6 degrees away) is detected as SP4; OCF7 (face-monocapped octahedron, SHAPE COC-7, 8.72) as TPRS7; SAPR8 (square antiprism, 9.65) and TPRS8 (square-face bicapped trigonal prism, 9.12) as DD8; OCT8 (trans-bicapped octahedron, 3.45) as HBPY8. TPRS7 is therefore the class of the capped seven-bond arrangements, and DD8 the class of the non-cubic eight-bond arrangements other than the bipyramid HBPY8 and the triangular-face bicapped prism TPRT8.

A person or another program can tell such shapes apart, so a supplied symbol may be any of _NAMED_SYMBOLS (protonet follow-up 8): every IUPAC polyhedral symbol and the SHAPE labels of the table, or I on a site with three or more bonds. A symbol from the baseline (software httk_basic) names a class, while a supplied symbol names the shape itself; the network’s source and shapes_source tell the two apart. I is never valid on two bonds (they are L2 or A2). I carries no bond count, which follows from the network (protonet follow-up 9): _SHAPE_SYMBOLS is the one list of symbols, in the code, labels, records and the exchange formats (OPTIMADE, CIF) alike, and a former I<n> is refused with a message to write I. The defaults (_DEFAULTS) for five, seven and eight bonds are the most frequent shapes on an ICSD sample (protonet follow-up 7): SPY5, TPRS7 and DD8.

[IUPAC]

N. G. Connelly, T. Damhus, R. M. Hartshorn, A. T. Hutton (eds.), Nomenclature of Inorganic Chemistry: IUPAC Recommendations 2005, RSC Publishing, Cambridge, 2005, section IR-9.3.2.1 and Table IR-9.2 (p. 176).

[SHAPE]

M. Llunell, D. Casanova, J. Cirera, P. Alemany, S. Alvarez, SHAPE: Program for the Stereochemical Analysis of Molecular Fragments by Means of Continuous Shape Measures and Associated Tools, User’s Manual, Version 2.1, Universitat de Barcelona, March 2013, “List of reference shapes” (pp. 7-9).

[Casanova2003]

D. Casanova, P. Alemany, J. M. Bofill, S. Alvarez, “Shape and symmetry of heptacoordinate transition-metal complexes: structural trends”, Chem. Eur. J. 9, 1281-1295 (2003).
